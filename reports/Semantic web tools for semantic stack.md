# Twelve additions close semantic-stack's SHACL, vocabulary and MCP gaps

**Bottom line:** the research (GitHub, Docker Hub, conference proceedings, W3C lists, practitioner newsletters, all read on 14 October 2026) finds no missing *triplestore* worth adding to semantic-stack by default, but it finds clear, low-risk gaps in four other layers. Add now, in the default `docker-compose.yml`: **dbcls/sparql-proxy** (cache, update-blocking, query log in front of QLever/Fuseki/Oxigraph), **Skosmos 3.4** (SKOS browser, official image, can reuse the existing Fuseki), **pySHACL 0.40 in server mode** plus **SHACL Play 0.12.5** (validation, SHACL documentation, UML, owl2shacl), **Sparklis** (zero-config visual query builder), **SPARQL Anything 1.3.0-RC2** (query CSV/JSON/XML/HTML as RDF), and a `tools` profile with **SDM-RDFizer** (100 % conformant on every new-RML-spec module in the 2026 KGCW challenge, unlike the pinned rmlmapper 7.3.3), **rudof** (ShEx+SHACL+DCTAP), **EYE** (N3 reasoning) and **jelly-cli**. Add as optional profiles: **Virtuoso OSE 7.2.17** and **GraphDB 11.5.1 free mode** (the two engines practitioners still ask for, with GPL and licence-key caveats), **Ontop 5.5.0** (virtual KG over SQL), **Sparnatural 13.1**, **Prez**, **grlc**, **pyLODE/WIDOCO**, and an `mcp` profile built around **open-ontologies** (1,054 stars, GHCR image) and **mcp-server-qlever**, with **sparql-llm** or **flexible-graphrag** for NL-to-SPARQL/GraphRAG experiments. The strongest external signals behind these choices are the 2026 KGCW conformance table, SHACL appearing as a named hiring requirement, the ISWC 2025/SEMANTiCS 2025 demo tracks (SHACL Dashboard, Jelly, GRASP, Skosmos workshop), Wikidata's 2026 decision to replace Blazegraph with QLever, and GraphDB's move to licence-gated free use followed by its 2026 private-equity takeover. The existing stack has drifted: Fuseki 5.5.0 sits two major lines behind Jena 6.2.0 (Java 21), RDF4J Workbench 5.2.2 behind 6.0.1 (Java 25, RDF 1.2/SPARQL 1.2), rmlmapper 7.3.3 behind 8.1.0 (Java 21), `yarrrml-parser:1.10.0` behind 1.12.2, and the `latest` tags on QLever and Oxigraph should be pinned to 0.6.0 and 0.5.11 respectively. LinkedIn posts were essentially unretrievable, so practitioner signal comes from Substacks, conference pages, mailing lists and one 2026 self-hosting blog.

## Prioritised shortlist: what to add, where, and on which port

The tables below are the decision. Host ports avoid everything already bound (3030, 3333, 7001, 7333, 7878, 8082-8088) and use 8089-8109 plus each tool's conventional port where that is free. "Maintenance" is the last release or push the researchers could verify; where a note says a figure was unverified, the table says so rather than guessing.

## Triplestores: Virtuoso and GraphDB free mode are the only engines worth a profile

The triplestore layer is where the stack is already strongest and where the field changed least. Across every maintained store with a Docker image, the researchers found no 2025-2026 "1.0" of a new open-source engine; the new entrants are pre-1.0 Rust projects created in mid-2026 with between **1 and 48 stars** ([Open Triplestore](https://github.com/philipperenzen/open-triplestore), [sparq](https://github.com/sparq-org/sparq), [PurRDF](https://github.com/Blackcat-Informatics/purrdf)). The stack's existing core, meanwhile, keeps gaining external validation: QLever reached **full SPARQL 1.1 conformance including Update and the Graph Store Protocol in June 2025** under an EU NGI Search grant, UniProt switched its endpoint from Virtuoso to QLever, and Wikimedia chose QLever in 2026 to replace Blazegraph for the Wikidata Query Service ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QLever), [NGI Search](https://ngi.eu/funded_solution/ngi-search34/)). Patel-Schneider's ISWC 2025 Wikidata-workshop benchmark ranked QLever fastest of Blazegraph, MillenniumDB, QLever and Virtuoso ([CEUR Vol-4108](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4108/paper3.pdf)), and Juan Sequeda relayed ISWC hallway talk that "QLever could become the DuckDB for RDF graphs" ([Sequeda trip report](https://juansequeda.substack.com/p/trip-report-iswc2025)).

That leaves two engines that users of a consulting stack will still ask for. **Virtuoso Open Source** is the other engine that passed Wikimedia's WDQS acceptance tests on WDBench and WGPB, and it has an official image with `7.2.17` tags, `VIRT_<SECTION>_<KEY>` environment overrides, an `/initdb.d` hook and the standard 8890/1111 ports ([Docker Hub](https://hub.docker.com/r/openlink/virtuoso-opensource-7), [WDQS report](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/47/WDQS_Triple_Store_Evaluation_-_Benchmark_Results_Report.pdf)). Its drawbacks are the GPL licence, SQL-side bulk loading and memory tuning, none of which matter for an opt-in profile. **GraphDB** is the harder call: it is the most practical proprietary addition (single `ontotext/graphdb` image for all editions, port 7200, RDF4J-compatible API, reasoning, SHACL, connectors with vector search since 11.2, built-in MCP since 11) ([Docker Hub](https://hub.docker.com/r/ontotext/graphdb/), [11.2.0 notes](https://help.graphwise.ai/en/graphdb/graphdb-release-notes/graphdb-11-2-0-release-notes.html)), but since GraphDB 11 "all editions (including GraphDB Free) require a license" ([rdflib docs](https://rdflib.readthedocs.io/en/stable/graphdb/)), and the exact Free-mode caps could not be retrieved from Graphwise's own pages; only a secondary analysis piece reports 5 repositories, 2 concurrent queries and a revenue-use restriction ([beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/oakley-capital-graphwise-acquisition-graphdb-license-not-open-source)). A GraphDB profile should therefore document the licence-file mount and leave the key to the user.

Two more server-side pieces belong in the infrastructure discussion even though they are not stores. **dbcls/sparql-proxy** is the single highest-value infrastructure addition because the stack's browser UIs currently hit endpoints directly: it validates queries, omits SPARQL Update by default, queues jobs with `MAX_CONCURRENCY`, caches to file/memory/Redis/memcached and logs queries to `QUERY_LOG_PATH` ([README](https://github.com/dbcls/sparql-proxy)). For GeoSPARQL, the cheapest route is not a new engine but a second Fuseki configured with Jena's GeoSPARQL assembler (OGC GeoSPARQL 1.0, six conformance classes) or Zazuko's `fuseki-geosparql` image, since QLever's spatial joins and Oxigraph's `spargeo` functions cover distance and bounding-box workloads but not the OGC conformance classes ([Jena GeoSPARQL](https://jena.apache.org/documentation/geosparql/), [Oxigraph README](https://github.com/oxigraph/oxigraph)). Monitoring is already native: Fuseki exposes `/$/metrics` for Prometheus and QLever added `/metrics` in v0.6.0 ([Fuseki docs](https://jena.apache.org/documentation/fuseki2/fuseki-server-info.html), [QLever releases](https://github.com/ad-freiburg/qlever/releases)).

## Ontology engineering: SHACL-first tooling has overtaken VOWL-style visualisers

The ontology layer is where the stack is thinnest (WebVOWL plus a reserved SHACL Playground) and where the research found the clearest shift in what the community builds. Upstream **WebVOWL is unmaintained as a product**: last release 1.1.6 in October 2019, the README warns the root Dockerfile is broken and the original domain is no longer owned by the project ([GitHub](https://github.com/VisualDataWeb/WebVOWL)). No drop-in VOWL-notation successor with a maintained image exists; the live visualisation projects are Ontodia's heirs (Reactodia v0.35.2, Zazuko graph-explorer 2.0.1, both libraries) and AWS graph-explorer v3.1.0, which has an official image and works with any SPARQL 1.1 endpoint but visualises instances rather than a class diagram ([Reactodia](https://github.com/reactodia/reactodia-workspace), [AWS docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/neptune/latest/userguide/visualization-graph-explorer.html)). The pragmatic answer is to keep the MetaCoding WebVOWL fork and add graph-explorer as a companion.

The centre of gravity has moved to **SHACL tooling**, and three signals say so at once. The W3C Data Shapes WG published at least ten SHACL 1.2 Core Working Drafts between January and August 2026 plus new Node Expressions (FPWD 8 Jan 2026) and User Interfaces (FPWD 26 May 2026) specs, though the 2017 SHACL is still the only Recommendation ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/WD-shacl12-core-20260828/), [W3C review announce](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-review-announce/2026May/0005.html)). 2026 job postings from HMH and Siemens Healthineers name SHACL validation next to SPARQL as a requirement ([HMH](https://careers.hmhco.com/job/Ontology-Engineer/1414025200/), [Siemens](https://jobs.siemens.com/en_US/externaljobs/JobDetail/499147)). And the active projects cluster there: pySHACL 0.40.x gained an HTTP server and an Oxigraph backend ([releases](https://github.com/RDFLib/pySHACL/releases)), SHACL Play shipped 0.12.3-0.12.5 between August and October 2026 ([releases](https://github.com/sparna-git/shacl-play/releases)), shacl-form released 3.7.2-3.7.4 in September-October 2026 ([releases](https://github.com/ULB-Darmstadt/shacl-form/releases.atom)), TopQuadrant SHACL 1.5.0 moved to Jena 6 with Jelly I/O ([releases](https://github.com/TopQuadrant/shacl/releases)), and rudof 0.3.10 covers ShEx, SHACL and DCTAP in one Rust binary ([GitHub](https://github.com/rudof-project/rudof)). Together pySHACL (service), SHACL Play (authoring: docs, UML, owl2shacl, JSON Schema), rudof (CLI) and the existing Playground (client-side check) give the stack a complete shape workflow; the ITB validator is the "productised" alternative for teams that want fixed application-profile domains ([ITB guide](https://www.itb.ec.europa.eu/docs/guides/latest/validatingRDF/index.html)).

Editing OWL in a browser remains the unsolved problem. **There is still no lightweight, officially dockerised, actively released open-source OWL editor**: WebProtégé's next-generation deployment is a Keycloak + MongoDB + RabbitMQ + MinIO microservice stack with `/etc/hosts` and manual SystemAdmin steps ([webprotege-deploy](https://github.com/protegeproject/webprotege-deploy)); VocBench 15.1.2 is the most capable editor but has no official image and nudges users toward GraphDB/RDF4J ([vocbench-user](https://groups.google.com/g/vocbench-user/c/pjIfpsgPC3E)); the new single-container entrants are either BSL-licensed (OrionBelt, 215 stars in eleven months) or very young (RDFArchitect, ProtegeDesk) ([OrionBelt](https://github.com/ralforion/orionbelt-ontology-builder), [RDFArchitect](https://github.com/SOPTIM/RDFArchitect)). Documentation generation is a solved and cheap add: pyLODE 3.6.0 ships a Falcon server with Dockerfile targets and `ontpub`/`vocpub`/`valpub` modes, and WIDOCO has a GHCR image ([pyLODE](https://github.com/RDFLib/pyLODE), [WIDOCO](https://github.com/dgarijo/Widoco)). Reasoning as an HTTP service is a dead category (Konclude's image is from 2021, Owlery's from 2022), so reasoning should enter the stack as CLI steps: EYE for N3, ROBOT for OWL profiles, Nemo for Datalog materialisation reading straight from the stack's SPARQL endpoints ([Konclude](https://hub.docker.com/v2/repositories/konclude/konclude/tags), [ROBOT reason](https://robot.obolibrary.org/reason), [Nemo](https://github.com/knowsys/nemo)). The one 2026 research tool worth watching here is OntoInk, which turns ontologies into a reasoned, SHACL-annotated MkDocs site with HermiT or Konclude-WASM, has a Dockerfile and an ISWC 2026 companion paper, but only a handful of stars ([arXiv 2610.05945](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05945)).

## Mapping and ETL: the 2026 KGCW table says the pinned rmlmapper is no longer the conformance leader

The KGCW 2026 challenge at ESWC (Dubrovnik, 10 May 2026) is the best neutral evidence for choosing a mapping engine, and it is unflattering to the stack's current pin. Across RML-Core, RML-IO, RML-CC, RML-FNML and RML-LV, **CARML (its unreleased `rml-cg` branch) and SDM-RDFizer scored 100 % on all five modules**, BURP-Error scored 100/95.89/100/100/100, Typhon-RML 86.84/86.30/0/0/0 and MappingWeaver 92.10/28.76/0/78.94/100 ([CEUR Vol-4245 paper16](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4245/paper16.pdf)). RMLMapper itself was reported in the 2025 edition as "largely" supporting RML-Core and RML-IO at 90 % and 65 % ([CEUR Vol-3999 short2](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3999/short2.pdf)). Since the KG Construction CG announced in January 2026 that it is moving toward a W3C Working Group to take RML to Recommendation ([public-kg-construct](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-kg-construct/2026Jan/0001.html)), a new-spec engine belongs in the `tools` profile next to rmlmapper. SDM-RDFizer is the practical pick because it has a published image updated 2026-09-10 and pip install ([Docker Hub](https://hub.docker.com/r/sdmtib/sdmrdfizer/tags)); CARML's conformant build is unreleased and Morph-KGC's Docker Hub image is stuck at v2.2.0 from April 2024 despite PyPI 2.10.0 in January 2026 with Jelly output and HTTP sources ([Morph-KGC tags](https://hub.docker.com/r/kgconstruct/morph-kgc/tags), [releases](https://github.com/morph-kgc/morph-kgc/releases)).

Two further mapping tools extend what the stack can ingest rather than how well. **Ontop 5.5.0** is the only mature open-source OBDA engine, exposing PostgreSQL/MySQL/DuckDB as a SPARQL endpoint without materialising triples; its official image was refreshed 2026-02-14 and 5.4.0 added a two-phase unfolder and `rdfs:subClassOf*` paths ([ontop-vkg.org](https://ontop-vkg.org/guide/releases.html)). **SPARQL Anything** covers the ad-hoc "query this JSON/CSV/HTML file as RDF" niche and is release-active, with two 1.3.0 release candidates on 8 October 2026 aligning to the W3C Data Façades CG specs ([releases](https://github.com/SPARQL-Anything/sparql.anything/releases)); it also makes TARQL (frozen at v1.2 since 2019) redundant ([TARQL releases](https://github.com/tarql/tarql/releases.atom)). For the YARRRML path the stack already documents, `yarrrml-parser` 1.12.x moved HTTP targets to `htv:headers`, while Docker Hub's `latest` still points at 1.10.0 ([releases](https://github.com/RMLio/yarrrml-parser/releases.atom), [Docker Hub](https://hub.docker.com/r/rmlio/yarrrml-parser/tags)). LinkML deserves a mention as a schema-first alternative: its official `linkml/linkml` image tracks PyPI (1.12.1 on 2026-10-13) and generates SHACL, ShEx, OWL and JSON Schema from one YAML schema; 1.12.0 added an OpenAPI generator and RDFC-1.0 canonical RDF output ([Docker Hub](https://hub.docker.com/r/linkml/linkml/tags), [releases](https://github.com/linkml/linkml/releases)).

For orchestration, three options are both active and container-ready. Zazuko's barnard59 (5.1.6, npm) and AKSW's RDF Processing Toolkit (`aksw/rpt:2.0.0-rc7`, SPARQL-sequence ETL plus an RML-to-SPARQL converter and a built-in server on 7531) cover scripted pipelines in Node and JVM respectively, while LinkedPipes ETL adds a GUI designer at the cost of four containers tracking `main` ([barnard59](https://github.com/zazuko/barnard59), [RPT](https://github.com/Scaseco/RdfProcessingToolkit), [LinkedPipes](https://github.com/linkedpipes/etl)). The genuinely new piece is **Jelly**, a Protocol-Buffers RDF streaming format with a June 2025 paper, a SEMANTiCS 2025 developers-workshop talk, drop-in Fuseki and RDF4J plugins (Jena 6 support since jelly-jvm 3.7.0) and consumers in Morph-KGC and TopQuadrant SHACL ([arXiv 2506.11298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11298), [CEUR Vol-4064](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4064/), [plugins guide](https://jelly-rdf.github.io/jelly-jvm/dev/getting-started-plugins/)). It is plumbing, not a service: two JARs and a CLI image make the Python mapping world and the JVM stores interoperate at speed. Interlinking tools (Silk, LIMES) and streaming frameworks (RMLStreamer, SANSA, UnifiedViews) are dormant or Spark-class and should stay out ([Silk tags](https://hub.docker.com/r/silkworkbench/silk-framework/tags), [RMLStreamer](https://github.com/RMLio/RMLStreamer)).

## Query, browse and vocabulary UIs: Skosmos, Sparklis and a proxy fill the user-facing gaps

The stack already covers raw querying (YASGUI, Comunica), dereferencing (Trifid) and graph browsing (Blueprint). What it lacks is a vocabulary browser, a query builder for non-SPARQL users, and an API layer. **Skosmos 3.x** is the obvious vocabulary addition: version 3.0 (10 Dec 2025) rewrote the front-end, 3.1 moved settings to environment variables "which eases Docker deployment", 3.2 slimmed the image build, and 3.4 shipped on 31 Aug 2026 with official `quay.io/natlibfi/skosmos` images ([releases](https://github.com/NatLibFi/Skosmos/releases), [wiki](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/NatLibFi/Skosmos/wiki/Install-Skosmos-with-Fuseki-in-Docker)). Its `Generic` dialect runs on plain SPARQL 1.1, so it can browse QLever or Oxigraph, while pointing it at the existing Fuseki with a jena-text index gives notation search and the full feature set ([Configuration wiki](https://github.com/NatLibFi/Skosmos/wiki/Configuration)). A 9 October 2026 self-hosting comparison summarised the vocabulary landscape as "Skosmos to publish, iQvoc to curate, VocBench to collaborate at scale" and pinned Fuseki 6.2.0 in its Skosmos compose ([pistack.xyz](https://www.pistack.xyz/posts/2026-10-09-self-hosted-skos-vocabulary-management-skosmos-iqvoc-vocbench/)). iQvoc 4.16.0 (Rails 8.1, Apache-2.0, compose with Postgres) is the matching lightweight editor, with the caveat that its data lives in PostgreSQL rather than a triplestore ([releases](https://github.com/innoq/iqvoc/releases)).

For query building, two mature, store-agnostic browser apps stand out and are cheap to host. **Sparklis** (Apache-2.0, official `sferre/sparklis` image on 8080) needs no configuration and takes an `endpoint` URL parameter ([README](https://github.com/sebferre/sparklis)); **Sparnatural** (LGPL-3.0, 13.1.0 on 24 Sep 2026, 313 stars, used in production at the BnF) is more powerful but is configured through a SHACL file per dataset and has no official image ([releases](https://github.com/sparna-git/Sparnatural/releases.atom), [docs](https://docs.sparnatural.eu/)). Both are client-side, so both need CORS or a proxy, which is the second reason sparql-proxy belongs in Tier 1: route YASGUI, Sparklis, Sparnatural and LD-Explorer through one proxy and get caching, update-blocking and logging for free. Rhizomer is the only faceted browser that explicitly added a QLever server type (Aug 2025) but needs its own API container and optional MariaDB ([commits](https://github.com/rhizomik/rhizomerAPI/commits/master.atom), [wiki](https://github.com/rhizomik/rhizomerAPI/wiki/Deploy-using-Docker)); Sampo-UI 4.0.0 (Sept 2026) is a portal framework rather than a drop-in ([README](https://github.com/SemanticComputing/sampo-ui)).

For APIs over the stores, **grlc 1.3.11** turns a folder of `.rq` files into Swagger-documented endpoints and **Prez 4.25.x** delivers profile-based Linked Data APIs (VocPrez, SpacePrez) with `SPARQL_ENDPOINT` as its only required setting ([grlc](https://hub.docker.com/r/clariah/grlc), [Prez README](https://github.com/RDFLib/prez)). Both map directly onto Kurt Cagle's October 2026 argument that agents should consume named, SHACL-described queries served deterministically rather than free-form LLM SPARQL ([Inference Engineer](https://inferenceengineer.substack.com/p/understanding-graph-data-mcps)). GraphQL over SPARQL remains thin: HyperGraphQL and GraphSPARQL are dormant since 2022, and the maintained route is GraphQL-LD inside Comunica, which the stack already ships ([HyperGraphQL](https://github.com/hypergraphql/hypergraphql), [comunica.dev](https://comunica.dev/)). Comunica 5.2 (14 Apr 2026) itself added a YASGUI-based HTML view to its endpoint, so the stack's Comunica service now has a UI without extra work ([comunica.dev blog](https://comunica.dev/blog/2026-04-14-release_5_2/)). A dereferencing front-end that is both maintained and endpoint-driven is a genuine gap: LodView is unmaintained since 2017/2023 (community image >4 years old) and LDView is a one-person 2026 prototype, so Trifid should stay the answer ([LodView](https://hub.docker.com/r/linkeddatacenter/lodview), [LDView](https://codeberg.org/renevoorburg/ldview)). Full-text search is likewise best served by the stores themselves (jena-text, QLever's `ql:contains-word`, RDF4J LuceneSail); no maintained generic "SPARQL to OpenSearch indexer with UI" was found, only DBpedia Lookup (last push Feb 2025) ([Jena text](https://jena.apache.org/documentation/query/text-query.html), [QLever text search](https://docs.qlever.dev/text-search/), [DBpedia Lookup](https://github.com/dbpedia/dbpedia-lookup)).

## LLM and MCP: attention has moved here, but almost every server is a one-person project

The 2026 star leaders in `topic:sparql`/`topic:rdf` are agent-oriented: open-ontologies (1,054 stars in seven months), cortexdb (381), ontobricks (411), TrustGraph (2,807), which signals demand for "MCP server over a triplestore" rather than for classic components ([open-ontologies](https://github.com/fabio-rovai/open-ontologies), [cortexdb](https://github.com/liliang-cn/cortexdb), [ontobricks](https://github.com/databrickslabs/ontobricks), [TrustGraph](https://github.com/trustgraph-ai/trustgraph)). Yet **there is no dominant SPARQL MCP server**: the generic candidates are rareba/sparql-mcp (read-only, stdio, 1 star, no image), Kashif-Rabbani/mcp-server-sparql (HTTP/SSE on 8000, the only one covering SPARQL Update, Graph Store Protocol and SHACL, which maps onto Fuseki's `/query`, `/update`, `/data` layout but exposes DROP/CLEAR), comunica-feature-mcp (official, six engines, no image) and XORwell/mcp-server-qlever (GHCR image, QLever autocomplete and query-plan tools, 2 stars) ([rareba](https://github.com/rareba/sparql-mcp), [Kashif-Rabbani](https://github.com/Kashif-Rabbani/mcp-server-sparql), [comunica](https://github.com/comunica/comunica-feature-mcp), [XORwell](https://github.com/XORwell/mcp-server-qlever)). The vendor-official servers are Wikimedia Deutschland's WikidataMCP (Dockerfile + compose, hosted at wd-mcp.wmcloud.org), Comunica's, Stardog Cloud's (SaaS-only) and GraphDB 11's built-in SSE endpoint at `/mcp/sse` (proprietary) ([WikidataMCP](https://github.com/wmde/WikidataMCP), [Graphwise docs](https://help.graphwise.ai/en/graphrag/graphrag-configuration-guide/graphwise-graphdb-integration--mcp-.html)). Zazuko, LinkML and SHACL have no dedicated MCP server ([zazuko](https://github.com/zazuko)). The practical recommendation is an `mcp` profile with open-ontologies (its own embedded Oxigraph, so no coupling to the stack's store) and mcp-server-qlever, documented as `claude mcp add` snippets, with every image pinned to a tag or commit because maintenance risk is high.

For natural-language querying, **sparql-llm** (SIB, MIT, 127 stars) is the reference: it ranked first among eight systems in the TEXT2SPARQL 2026 challenge at ESWC with up to 59 % higher accuracy than the runner-up, and ships a compose deployment with a chat UI on 8000, Qdrant on 6333 and a Streamable-HTTP MCP server ([arXiv 2610.13648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13648), [README](https://github.com/sib-swiss/sparql-llm)). The cost is real: it needs VoID descriptions and SHACL-described example queries per endpoint and documents only OpenAI-compatible APIs, so Claude would go through a gateway. GRASP from the QLever group (ISWC 2025, WikiKGQA 2026 winner, defaults to QLever endpoints, supports Anthropic) is the lighter alternative ([GRASP README](https://github.com/ad-freiburg/grasp)). For RDF GraphRAG, **flexible-graphrag** is the most direct fit: Fuseki/GraphDB/Oxigraph backends, compose includes on the same ports the stack uses (3030, 7878), native Anthropic and Ollama providers and an MCP server, at the price of a heavy LlamaIndex + LangChain + Qdrant dependency surface ([README](https://github.com/stevereiner/flexible-graphrag)). TrustGraph is a whole platform (Cassandra, Pulsar/RabbitMQ, Qdrant) and would be a second stack ([README](https://github.com/trustgraph-ai/trustgraph)). Hybrid vector search cannot come from the stores themselves: Apache Jena closed the `jena-vector` proposal as not planned on 2025-06-08, and no Oxigraph or QLever vector support was found, so any hybrid retrieval needs an external vector DB ([apache/jena#3193](https://github.com/apache/jena/issues/3193)). For text-to-RDF extraction, OntoGPT/SPIRES (BSD-3, 1,051 stars, PyPI 1.2.0 Sept 2026, LiteLLM so Anthropic and Ollama work, emits RDF/OWL against a LinkML schema) is the only mature, provider-agnostic choice, though it has no Docker image ([README](https://github.com/monarch-initiative/ontogpt)). LangChain's `GraphSparqlQAChain` has moved to the deprecated-lookup path and LlamaIndex has pivoted to property graphs, so neither is a basis for new work ([langchain-classic](https://reference.langchain.com/python/langchain-classic/chains/graph_qa/sparql/DEPRECATED_LOOKUP), [LlamaIndex blog](https://llamaindex.ai/blog/introducing-the-property-graph-index-a-powerful-new-way-to-build-knowledge-graphs-with-llms)).

## Community, conference and standards signals that justify the choices

Conference demo and resource tracks in 2025 point to the same three categories the stack lacks. The ISWC 2025 companion volume (Nara, Nov 2025) includes the SHACL Dashboard demo, GRASP, LOAMA, Q²Forge and metaphacts' metis agent platform, and cube.link (Zazuko's RDF cube schema) won Best In-Use Paper ([CEUR Vol-4085](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4085/), [Sequeda](https://juansequeda.substack.com/p/trip-report-iswc2025)). SEMANTiCS 2025 (Vienna, Sept 2025) surfaced Jelly, Miravi, RDF Differ, TermIt and the Nobel Prize Sampo-UI demo ([CEUR Vol-4064](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4064/)), and SWIB25 ran hands-on workshops on Skosmos, Metafacture and Annif plus talks on LinkedDataHub and Shacl4Bib ([SWIB25 programme](https://forum.swib.org/t/swib25-programme/934)). ESWC 2026 accepted posters/demos include Comunica MCP SPARQL, rudof-MCP and a web-based OWLGrEd ([ESWC 2026 list](https://2026.eswc-conferences.org/program/accepted-papers/poster-demo)). The pattern is SHACL reporting, vocabulary publishing and MCP, exactly the Tier 1 and Tier 2 additions above. Among awesome-lists, `ozekik/awesome-ontology` (719 stars, pushed 12 Oct 2026) is the most actively curated and, against `semantalytics/awesome-semantic-web`, the stack's missing categories were a SHACL form/report UI, an RDF diff tool, a SKOS server, an ontology documentation generator and a rule engine ([awesome-ontology](https://github.com/ozekik/awesome-ontology), [awesome-semantic-web](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/semantalytics/awesome-semantic-web/master/README.md)).

Standards activity frames the "drift" items below. RDF 1.2 Concepts and Semantics reached Candidate Recommendation on 7 April 2026 with triple terms and directional language strings, while SPARQL 1.2 Query was last seen as a January 2026 Working Draft ([W3C review announce](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-review-announce/2026Apr/0003.html), [SPARQL 1.2 WD](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/WD-sparql12-query-20260123/)). The implementations that signal 1.2 support are already in the stack: Oxigraph behind its `rdf-12` feature flag with RDFC-1.0 canonicalization and JSON-LD 1.1 by default, Jena 6, and RDF4J 6.0 ([Oxigraph releases](https://github.com/oxigraph/oxigraph/releases), [RDF4J 6.0.0 notes](https://rdf4j.org/release-notes/6.0.0)). The actionable item is therefore documenting how to enable 1.2 modes, not adding engines. On the vendor side, Zazuko remains the richest open-source source and most of its catalogue beyond Trifid and Blueprint (barnard59, cube-creator/cube-viewer, SHACL Playground, RDF Sketch, SPEX, graph-explorer, prefix-server, kopflos, `varnish-post` POST-caching, `fuseki-geosparql`, Helm charts) is Docker-friendly ([zazuko.com/tools](https://zazuko.com/tools/), [varnish-post](https://github.com/zazuko/varnish-post)). GraphDB's licence gating in 2025 and Oakley Capital's majority takeover of Graphwise on 19 Aug 2026 prompted at least one widely shared analysis to recommend Jena/Fuseki, QLever and RDF4J as the open alternatives, which is exactly semantic-stack's core and a useful positioning line ([beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/oakley-capital-graphwise-acquisition-graphdb-license-not-open-source)).

Practitioner voices converge on governance over generation. Sequeda's March 2026 Substack cites Gartner data that 44 % of organisations implemented a semantic layer in 2025 but only one in seven governs it ([Sequeda](https://juansequeda.substack.com/p/gartner-data-and-analytics-march)); Cagle's October 2026 "Understanding Graph Data MCPs" recommends SHACL input/output shapes per named query and staged SPARQL Update into candidate graphs ([Inference Engineer](https://inferenceengineer.substack.com/p/understanding-graph-data-mcps)); Dean Allemang's Connected Data London 2026 talk is titled "Steering Vibe Ontologies with Ontologist Judgment" ([CDL 2026](https://2026.connected-data.london/)); and Tony Seale's organisation shipped Vault-LD (Markdown vaults as linked data, Apache-2.0, 239 stars) ([GitHub](https://github.com/The-Knowledge-Graph-Guys/vault-ld)). Each supports adding validation and named-query tooling (SHACL services, grlc, Prez) alongside any MCP or LLM front-end.

## Drift in the existing stack: four major-version gaps and two unpinned tags

The research turned up as much about what the stack already ships as about what it lacks. **Fuseki** is pinned to `secoresearch/fuseki:5.5.0` (Jena 5.5.0, 14 Jul 2025) while Jena has tagged 6.0.0 (27 Jan 2026), 6.1.0 (3 May 2026) and 6.2.0 (27 Jul 2026); Jena 6 requires Java 21 and retires the Fuseki WAR and jena-permissions, so the upgrade is deliberate rather than a tag bump ([Jena tags](https://github.com/apache/jena/tags), [download page](https://jena.apache.org/download/), [5.6.0 CHANGES](https://apache.googlesource.com/jena/+/5b54b3c8e341233f9a100fc0ae8fa801d172d400%5E%21)). **RDF4J Workbench** is pinned to 5.2.2 while 6.0.0 (2 Jul 2026) and 6.0.1 (20 Aug 2026) bring RDF 1.2 and SPARQL 1.2, require Java 25, move to Jakarta Servlet/Tomcat 11 and remove the Solr SAIL ([rdf4j.org](https://rdf4j.org/), [6.0.0 notes](https://rdf4j.org/release-notes/6.0.0)). **rmlmapper-java** is pinned to v7.3.3 (Apr 2025) while v8.0.1 (11 Dec 2025) and v8.1.0 (23 Dec 2025, Java 21, Maven Central) exist; Docker Hub's newest tag seen was v8.0.1 ([Zenodo v8.1.0](https://zenodo.org/records/18032528), [Docker Hub](https://hub.docker.com/r/rmlio/rmlmapper-java/tags)). The README's `rmlio/yarrrml-parser:1.10.0` is behind 1.12.2 (Oct 2025), and the `latest` tag on Docker Hub itself still points at 1.10.0 ([releases](https://github.com/RMLio/yarrrml-parser/releases.atom), [Docker Hub](https://hub.docker.com/r/rmlio/yarrrml-parser/tags)). **OpenRefine** is pinned to 3.5.0, but the RDF Transform extension (2.3.6, Sept 2025) has release notes that reference OpenRefine 3.8+ ([releases](https://github.com/AtesComp/rdf-transform/releases)).

Two services run on `latest`. **QLever** publishes version-tagged images since v0.5.50 and v0.6.0 (13 Aug 2026) adds runtime index swap via `rebuild-index`, Prometheus `/metrics` and CONSTRUCT dedup, so `adfreiburg/qlever:0.6.0` is the sensible pin ([releases](https://github.com/ad-freiburg/qlever/releases), [Docker Hub](https://hub.docker.com/r/adfreiburg/qlever)). **Oxigraph** 0.5.11 (2 Sep 2026) is current; 0.5.8 introduced a new `/sparql` path for query and update and "a significant set of new" GeoSPARQL functions, 0.5.5 enabled JSON-LD 1.1 by default, and RDF 1.2/SPARQL 1.2 stay behind the `rdf-12` flag, so the `docker-compose.oxigraph.yml` endpoint comments (`/query`, `/update`) should mention `/sparql` and the image should be pinned ([releases](https://github.com/oxigraph/oxigraph/releases), [CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oxigraph/oxigraph/main/CHANGELOG.md)). On the licence side, the stack contains no GraphDB, but OntoRefine is Ontotext's and GraphDB's 2025 move to licence-gated Free use plus the 2026 Graphwise takeover is worth a README note for users who pair OntoRefine with GraphDB ([rdflib docs](https://rdflib.readthedocs.io/en/stable/graphdb/), [beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/oakley-capital-graphwise-acquisition-graphdb-license-not-open-source)). Comunica 5.2 added a YASGUI view to its endpoint ([comunica.dev](https://comunica.dev/blog/2026-04-14-release_5_2/)), and upstream WebVOWL's last release remains 1.1.6 from 2019 with a broken root Dockerfile, which justifies keeping the MetaCoding fork ([GitHub](https://github.com/VisualDataWeb/WebVOWL)).

## Limits of this research

The brief asked for practitioner signal from LinkedIn, and that was largely not obtainable: every `site:linkedin.com` query returned non-LinkedIn pages, and no post text could be retrieved for any named practitioner (Sequeda, Seale, Cagle, Allemang, Lassila, Barrasa, Gschwend, Francart, Verborgh, Bast and others); the only LinkedIn URLs in the notes are a 2023 Sequeda benchmark post and a c. 2023 Allemang post ([Sequeda LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/juansequeda_investing-in-knowledge-graph-provides-higher-activity-7130207789620101120-_TUM), [Allemang LinkedIn](https://ci.linkedin.com/posts/dean-allemang-96218_llms-closing-the-kg-gap-activity-7044687238600740864-Ge18)). Practitioner signal was instead reconstructed from Substacks (Sequeda, Cagle's Inference Engineer and The Ontologist), the Year of the Graph newsletter, conference programmes (ISWC 2025, SEMANTiCS 2025, SWIB25, ESWC 2026, CDL 2025/2026, BFH Linked Data Meetups), the KGC blog, W3C mailing lists, the pistack.xyz self-hosting comparison, job postings and a third-party r/semanticweb statistics page that captures only that "Best RDF triplestore?" is a recurring question, not the answers ([gummysearch](https://gummysearch.com/r/semanticweb/)). Hacker News surfaced only two low-traction RDF stories since January 2025 ([HN Algolia](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=SPARQL%20RDF&tags=story&numericFilters=created_at_i%3E1735689600&hitsPerPage=40)).

Other gaps the reader should weigh: GitHub's per-repo API was blocked in several sessions, so star counts and push dates come from the GitHub search API, the ecosyste.ms mirror, Docker Hub's v2 API, PyPI and npm, and should be read as approximate; several GitHub release pages print day and month only, so years for Community Solid Server 7.2.0, OLS4 4.0.x, RDF Delta and Trellis are inferred. Ports for Ontop (8080), SPARQL Anything (3000), the ITB validator (8080) and open-ontologies' HTTP mode were not re-verified from primary pages. Licences were not confirmed from primary text for Skosmos (GitHub NOASSERTION), VocBench, Virtuoso (NOASSERTION), Zazuko graph-explorer, barnard59, LinkedPipes and Elda. GraphDB Free's exact limits, Jena 6's RDF 1.2 status, the KGCW 2025 per-engine results, Nemo's GHCR tag naming and whether Prez has been tested against QLever or Oxigraph could not be verified. Accepted-paper lists for the ISWC 2025 and ESWC 2026 resource tracks, KGC 2026 and SEMANTiCS 2026 (Ghent, Sept 2026) were not retrievable, so no tool is attributed to those tracks except where a paper's own page says so.

## Conclusion

The most useful reframing from this research is that semantic-stack's competitive edge in late 2026 is no longer "which triplestore" but "which contracts": SHACL shapes, named queries and MCP tools are what practitioners, hiring managers and conference demos now treat as the product surface over a graph, and the stack's three open stores (QLever, Fuseki, Oxigraph) are precisely the trio that the GraphDB licence episode pushed people toward. The Tier 1 list is deliberately small, open-licensed and mostly official-image, because the one consistent finding across every category is that the interesting new projects (MCP servers, Rust engines, browser editors) are one-maintainer efforts with single-digit to low-hundreds stars; the right way to adopt them is as pinned, opt-in profiles behind a proxy, not as defaults.

Two follow-ups would pay off quickly. First, a stack-specific Claude Code skill describing endpoints, ports, prefixes and an "introspect, draft, validate with pySHACL, run through sparql-proxy" loop would deliver more than any third-party skill found, all of which are generic and sub-five-star ([semantic-web-reasoner-skill](https://github.com/chimezie/semantic-web-reasoner-skill)). Second, the major-version upgrades (Jena 6/Java 21, RDF4J 6/Java 25, rmlmapper 8/Java 21) should be done as one coordinated change with Jelly plugins dropped in at the same time, since all three now share JDK 21+ baselines and Jelly's 3.7 line already targets Jena 6 and RDF4J 5.3 ([jelly-jvm releases](https://github.com/Jelly-RDF/jelly-jvm/releases)).
